INSIDE THE TRAINING EXPERIENCE

From foundational skills to focused expertise.

Build procedural confidence, deepen your subspecialty knowledge, and learn alongside faculty across the Mount Sinai system.

Inpatient consult service

Learn to evaluate acute GI conditions and guide treatment through daily bedside rounds. Work with referring physicians, radiologists, and surgeons to coordinate care as part of a multidisciplinary team.

Endoscopic training

Develop diagnostic and therapeutic skills in upper endoscopy, colonoscopy, sigmoidoscopy, PEG placement, and band ligation under direct supervision. Inpatient procedures build early experience; dedicated outpatient endoscopy rotations in the second and third years provide further opportunities to develop technique.

Continuity clinic, shaped around you

First-year fellows sample clinics across all training sites before selecting a general GI clinic and a hepatology clinic for their longitudinal experience. This early breadth helps you choose the settings where you will build lasting patient relationships and develop confidence in outpatient care. Faculty supervision and capped clinic volumes support thoughtful care, while subspecialty rotations add focused outpatient experience.

Transplant hepatology

During the second year, fellows spend four weeks immersed in transplant hepatology at The Mount Sinai Hospital. Working alongside the transplant team, you develop a deeper understanding of complex liver disease and the care of patients before and after transplantation—connecting clinical decisions with the full transplant journey.

Hepatobiliary diseases & advanced endoscopy

Second- and third-year fellows explore pancreatic and biliary disease through concentrated exposure to clinical decision-making, EUS, and ERCP. Procedural participation grows with individual skill; this experience complements, but does not replace, a fourth-year advanced endoscopy fellowship.

Inflammatory bowel disease

A dedicated rotation at The Mount Sinai Hospital combines outpatient and inpatient consultation, procedures, and IBD conference participation, with one-on-one faculty teaching. Fellows complete at least one month during the second or third year and present a case or review topic at IBD conference.

Neurogastroenterology & motility

Mount Sinai West is home to the Mount Sinai Health System’s motility center. Fellows train alongside specialists in neurogastroenterology, gaining experience with complex functional and motility disorders and the interpretation of manometry, reflux monitoring, gastric emptying, and anorectal testing. Second- and third-year rotations emphasize choosing appropriate studies, recognizing interpretive limitations, and understanding pelvic floor biofeedback.

Conferences & professional development

Weekly grand rounds and a weekly academic half day give fellows dedicated time to learn together. Regular esophageal conferences complement case discussions, journal club, pathology, radiology, board review, and endoscopy quality improvement. Fellows also participate in NYSGE intercity rounds and its annual hands-on course.

All first-year fellows attend the ASGE First Year Fellows course, with expenses reimbursed and time excused from clinical duties.

Clinical nutrition, electives & research

Clinical nutrition is part of the rotation curriculum, alongside protected time for electives and research. Fellows build an understanding of malnutrition and enteral and parenteral feeding while developing their scholarly work.

A supported start to fellowship

First-year fellows receive time off for their internal medicine boards and do not take call until after boards are completed in September. First-year fellows also attend the NYSGE and ASGE First Year Fellows courses.

High expectations.
An open door.

“We expect excellence and hard work from the fellows, yet foster a nurturing and collegial environment”

Welcome to the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai (Mount Sinai Morningside and Mount Sinai West) Gastroenterology and Hepatology Fellowship Program. I am pleased that you are interested in learning more about our excellent program. We have a strong roster of dedicated faculty who are leaders in the field and yet are available at all times with open doors to our trainees.

We expect excellence and hard work from the fellows, yet foster a nurturing and collegial environment with focus on the quality of the training experience. Our fellows are happy and proud to be part of this community and play active roles in house staff education and patient care at our three teaching sites. 05 / LIVING ON THE WEST SIDE

Your New York chapter
starts here.

A neighborhood that makes room for life beyond the hospital.

Start the day with a walk through Central Park, finish with a run along the Hudson River, or head downtown for dinner in Chelsea. Midtown West puts the Upper West Side, Lincoln Center, and the waterfront within easy reach—and the rest of New York a subway ride away.

Fellows have lived in Queens and New Jersey, too. Most prefer hospital housing for its subsidized prices, short commute, and built-in community.

SUBSIDIZED HOUSING

A home close to everything.

Hospital housing is guaranteed for our fellows.

Both residences are right next to Mount Sinai West, with Columbus Circle, Central Park, Lincoln Center, the West Side Highway, and the Hudson River waterfront a short walk away. Pick up groceries at Whole Foods or Trader Joe’s, find a favorite coffee shop in Hell’s Kitchen or the Upper West Side, or spend an evening at Lincoln Center, Carnegie Hall, or a Broadway show. MoMA, neighborhood restaurants, and waterfront running and cycling routes offer plenty to explore on days off. Nearby subway connections make Chelsea, downtown Manhattan, and the rest of the city easy to reach.
